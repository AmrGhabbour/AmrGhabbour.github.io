Customer Lifetime Value Analysis

A predictive customer analytics project that estimates future purchasing behavior and lifetime value, then turns those predictions into actionable customer segments.

BUSINESS PROBLEM

Which customers are likely to create the most future value?

Historical revenue tells you what a customer has already generated. It does not directly answer what that customer is likely to generate next. This project builds a predictive CLV workflow so a business can move from backward-looking customer value to forward-looking decisions around segmentation, marketing and retention.

DECISION FRAMEWORK

Predict behavior first. Segment customers second.

The analysis separates the problem into two questions: how often a customer is expected to purchase, and how valuable each expected purchase is. Those predictions are then combined into Customer Lifetime Value and used as inputs to KMeans clustering.

DATA & PREPARATION

From transaction history to customer-level behavior

The notebook reads sales_transactions.csv. The dataset contains invoice, date, customer, item, quantity, item price and transaction value fields. Using the lifetimes library, the transaction history is transformed into customer-level metrics: frequency, recency, customer age/tenure (T), and monetary value.

ANALYTICAL PIPELINE

Four layers from raw transactions to customer strategy

01 · RFM foundation

Aggregate transaction history into customer-level frequency, recency, T and monetary value.

02 · Purchase prediction

Fit BetaGeoFitter to estimate each customer's expected future transaction activity.

03 · Value prediction

Fit GammaGammaFitter to estimate expected average monetary value per transaction.

04 · CLV segmentation

Combine the predictions into CLV, then use KMeans to group customers by predicted behavior and value.

MODEL DESIGN

Two predictive models, one customer-value view

The notebook uses a Beta-Geometric/Negative Binomial approach through BetaGeoFitter for future purchase frequency and GammaGammaFitter for expected transaction value. Both models are fitted with a penalizer coefficient of 0.01.

The BG/NBD model is used to estimate the expected number of purchases over a 30-day period and also calculates a customer-level probability of being alive. The Gamma-Gamma model estimates expected average transaction value.

The notebook then combines the two models through the customer_lifetime_value() function using a 12-month horizon, daily frequency and a 1% discount rate.

KEY INSIGHTS

The output is not just a CLV number. It is a customer prioritization framework.

  • Future purchase behavior: customers are ranked using predicted transaction activity rather than historical frequency alone.
  • Expected economic value: predicted transaction value adds a monetary dimension to future purchase behavior.
  • Customer health: the model calculates probability-alive, providing a behavioral signal for retention prioritization.
  • Actionable segmentation: KMeans clusters customers using predicted transactions, predicted transaction value and CLV.

CUSTOMER SEGMENTATION

Turning predictions into personas for action

Rather than clustering customers only on historical RFM values, the notebook clusters on pred_num_txn, pred_txn_value and CLV. The number of clusters is selected using Yellowbrick's KElbowVisualizer, after which KMeans assigns each customer to a segment.

This creates a practical bridge from predictive modeling to commercial action: high predicted value customers can be prioritized for retention and loyalty initiatives, while lower-value or lower-activity groups can be approached with more targeted and cost-conscious interventions.

BUSINESS APPLICATION

Where this analysis can support decisions

Retention

Prioritize customers with strong predicted future value and weaker recent engagement for proactive retention treatment.

Marketing allocation

Use predicted CLV to differentiate investment levels instead of treating every customer as equally valuable.

Loyalty strategy

Identify customers whose expected future contribution justifies stronger loyalty and relationship-building programs.

Customer portfolio management

Use the resulting clusters as a starting point for different commercial strategies across customer value tiers.
